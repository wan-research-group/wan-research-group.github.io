Join Us

Build the computing systems behind physical intelligence

The EMBARC Lab is a new group at Columbia, which means joining now puts you at the ground floor: close mentorship, first-author opportunities from day one, and a real say in the lab's direction. We work across computer architecture, VLSI/chip design, systems, and embodied & agentic AI, with close industry collaboration (IBM, TSMC, Intel, Google) and access to real silicon tapeouts.

What we look for

There is no single mold. Great fits for the lab usually have strength in one of these, plus curiosity about the rest:

  • Architecture / VLSI: accelerator design, SoC/RTL, compute-in-memory, FPGA prototyping, EDA.
  • Systems: ML systems, serving/runtime, compilers, performance characterization, benchmarking.
  • Embodied & agentic AI: robotics computing, LLM agents, neuro-symbolic methods, reinforcement learning.

Strong programming skills and intellectual honesty matter more than a perfect CV. We especially value students who like to build things end-to-end and measure them honestly.

Open positions

PhD Students

Multiple fully funded PhD positions for Fall 2027 (applications due December 2026). You will define and own research agendas across our four thrusts, publish at top venues (ISCA/MICRO/ASPLOS/HPCA/DAC/ISSCC…), and can be involved in chip tapeouts.

How: Apply to the Columbia CS PhD program and mention Prof. Zishen Wan in your application. Feel free to also email us (see below).

Postdoctoral Researchers

Openings for postdocs with a strong record in computer architecture, VLSI, ML systems, or embodied AI. Start date flexible; you will help shape a brand-new lab and mentor its first students.

How: Email your CV, a short research statement, and two representative papers.

Master's & Undergraduate Researchers

Columbia MS/undergraduate students can join for research projects, independent study, or thesis work. Strong projects regularly turn into publications and PhD recommendations.

How: Email your CV, transcript, and a short note on which research thrust excites you and why.

Visiting Scholars & Interns

We welcome motivated visiting students, scholars, and remote collaborators. Tell us what you want to build and how it connects to the lab's research.

How: Email your CV and a one-paragraph project idea.

How to reach us

Email zw3306@columbia.edu with the subject line [EMBARC + <position>] Your Name, for example [EMBARC PhD] Ada Lovelace.

To help us respond quickly, please attach your CV, mention 1-2 papers or projects of yours (or ours) you'd like to talk about, and say a sentence about why this lab specifically. We read every genuine email; personalized notes get answered first.

FAQ

  • Do I need prior tapeout or robotics experience? No. Depth in one relevant area and willingness to learn across layers is enough.
  • Can I work with you if I'm not at Columbia? Yes, we take remote collaborators and visiting students on a project basis.
  • Which PhD program should I apply to? Computer Science. If your background is EE/circuits, that's welcome, just mention it in your statement.
  • Is funding available? All PhD positions are fully funded (tuition + stipend).
